# Cross-Layer Hypotheses

Status: candidate hypotheses only.

## CLH-01 — Bit/network metrics versus experiential utility

Networking studies frequently emphasize latency, reliability, rate, and resource efficiency, while XR studies emphasize QoE, rendering quality, successful delivery, or experience. Semantic-communication methods introduce meaning/task utility. The review will test whether these metric families are evaluated jointly often enough to support cross-layer design conclusions.

## CLH-02 — Semantic efficiency versus compute cost

Semantic communication may reduce transmitted information while increasing encoder/decoder/model computation. The review will test whether Metaverse-oriented semantic communication studies jointly account for communication savings, semantic/task fidelity, inference cost, training cost, and end-to-end latency.

## CLH-03 — Adaptation under nonstationarity

Metaverse workloads combine mobility, wireless variation, user interaction, scene changes, digital-twin updates, and heterogeneous tasks. The review will test whether methods are evaluated under matched distribution shifts and whether adaptation cost is reported.

## CLH-04 — XR fidelity versus network/edge sustainability

Higher visual fidelity can increase bitrate, rendering demand, compute demand, and energy. The review will test whether visual quality/QoE and energy/carbon/resource cost are jointly measured under common scenarios.

## CLH-05 — Synchronization freshness as a missing bridge variable

Digital-twin and physical-virtual synchronization error/freshness may connect networking latency, edge allocation, sensing, and application correctness. The review will test whether freshness is explicitly modeled across these streams or remains siloed.

## CLH-06 — Multimodal semantic benchmark gap

The Metaverse is inherently multimodal, while mature semantic-communication baselines often specialize in text, speech, image, or task-specific modalities. The review will test whether reproducible multimodal, interactive Metaverse semantic benchmarks exist with shared task, QoE, latency, and compute metrics.
