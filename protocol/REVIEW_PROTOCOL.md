# Review Protocol

## Objective

Develop a comprehensive, reproducible, and tutorially useful synthesis of the networked Metaverse from foundational work to the current research frontier.

## Primary research questions

**RQ1. Foundations and evolution**  
How have definitions, architectural models, and enabling technologies of the Metaverse evolved?

**RQ2. System architecture**  
Which communication, networking, computing, digital-twin, XR, AI, identity, security, and interoperability components are required, and how are they connected?

**RQ3. Problems and methods**  
Which technical problems are studied, which method and algorithm families address them, and under what assumptions?

**RQ4. Evidence**  
What forms of evidence support reported contributions: conceptual analysis, simulation, prototype, public dataset evaluation, testbed, deployment, independent reproduction, or cross-system benchmark?

**RQ5. Comparability**  
Which studies can be compared fairly after accounting for workload, dataset, hardware, network conditions, baselines, metrics, and experimental protocol?

**RQ6. Reproducibility**  
Which studies provide code, data, configurations, seeds, environment information, and sufficient procedural detail for reproduction?

**RQ7. Gaps**  
Where do knowledge gaps, evidence gaps, comparison gaps, cross-layer gaps, benchmark gaps, and unresolved trade-offs remain?

**RQ8. Standards and deployment**  
How do research assumptions and proposed architectures align with relevant standards, interoperability efforts, and practical deployments?

**RQ9. Research agenda**  
Which open problems are sufficiently supported by evidence to define concrete, testable future research questions?

## Review orientation

The review is not organized as a catalogue of applications. It follows the chain:

Foundations → Architecture → Capabilities → Problems → Methods/Algorithms → Datasets → Metrics → Evidence → Comparability → Reproducibility → Gaps → Research Directions.

## Evidence maturity

- E0 — position, vision, or concept
- E1 — architecture or analytical model
- E2 — algorithm or method specification
- E3 — simulation
- E4 — prototype or testbed
- E5 — evaluation on public/curated data
- E6 — operational or real-world deployment
- E7 — independent reproduction
- E8 — normalized cross-system benchmark

Evidence level describes the form of validation and is not itself a quality score.

## Gap classes

- GK — knowledge gap
- GE — evidence gap
- GC — comparison/benchmark gap
- GX — cross-layer integration gap
- GT — unresolved trade-off gap
- GR — reproducibility gap
- GS — standards/deployment gap

## Gap validation rule

A candidate gap must not enter the final research agenda solely because it appears in an author's future-work section. It requires corroboration from the review corpus and explicit checking for contrary or newer evidence.

## Algorithm-development rule

A new algorithm is considered only after the review and normalized baseline analysis identify a specific unresolved algorithmic limitation. The new method must then be compared with relevant baselines under a common experimental protocol.
