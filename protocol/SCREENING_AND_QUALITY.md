# Screening and Quality Protocol

## Inclusion criteria

Include a study when it materially contributes to at least one review question and provides sufficient information to classify its problem, method, evidence, architecture, dataset/testbed, metric, standard, deployment, or research limitation.

Priority is given to peer-reviewed journal and conference literature. Standards and authoritative technical specifications are retained in a separate evidence class.

## Exclusion criteria

Exclude:

- records that only use "metaverse" rhetorically without a material technical or system contribution;
- duplicate versions when a later peer-reviewed version supersedes a preprint and no version-specific analysis is needed;
- editorials, news, promotional material, and unsupported opinion from the primary evidence pool;
- studies whose full contribution cannot be established from accessible evidence;
- application papers with no transferable technical, empirical, architectural, or evaluation insight when outside the defined scope.

## Screening stages

S1 — metadata deduplication  
S2 — title/abstract relevance  
S3 — full-text eligibility  
S4 — evidence extraction  
S5 — quality/reproducibility audit  
S6 — cross-check by another agent for gap-bearing studies

## Quality dimensions

Quality is recorded dimensionally rather than collapsed into a single score.

- Q1 problem definition clarity
- Q2 method/algorithm specification
- Q3 experimental design adequacy
- Q4 baseline appropriateness
- Q5 metric appropriateness
- Q6 dataset/workload transparency
- Q7 environment/configuration transparency
- Q8 statistical support
- Q9 artifact/code/data availability
- Q10 claim–evidence alignment
- Q11 limitations/threats-to-validity reporting
- Q12 standards/deployment relevance when applicable

Values: yes / partial / no / not-applicable / unclear.

## Gap-bearing study rule

Any study used to justify a major open problem must receive:

- bibliographic verification;
- full-text or sufficiently complete primary-source inspection;
- explicit evidence extraction;
- contrary-evidence search;
- second-pass validation.

No major gap is accepted from abstract-only evidence.
