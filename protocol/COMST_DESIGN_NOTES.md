# COMST-Oriented Review Design Notes

## Target venue

Primary target: IEEE Communications Surveys & Tutorials.

The review should be designed as a communications/networking survey with tutorial value, not as a general-purpose catalogue of all Metaverse applications.

## Structural implications

The final synthesis should:

- trace the field from foundational concepts to the current state and beyond;
- remain comprehensible to communications researchers outside the immediate Metaverse specialty;
- use figures and tables extensively to explain architectures, taxonomies, comparisons, lessons learned, and open problems;
- avoid unnecessary mathematical density;
- distinguish clearly between survey synthesis and any original computational benchmarking;
- prioritize reader utility, technical correctness, non-redundancy, and timeliness;
- fit the initial-submission page constraint.

## Proposed final synthesis flow

1. Scope, motivation, and review need
2. Definitions and historical evolution
3. Reference architecture for the networked Metaverse
4. Communication and computing requirements
5. Enabling technology families
6. Algorithmic problem families
7. Datasets, simulators, testbeds, and platforms
8. Comparative evidence by problem family
9. Reproducibility and benchmarkability
10. Security, privacy, identity, and trust
11. Interoperability and standards
12. Cross-layer trade-offs and contradictions
13. Evidence-supported open research problems
14. Lessons learned and research methodology guidance
15. Conclusion

## Review-comparison requirement

Before finalizing the taxonomy, compare the proposed coverage against the closest surveys and record:

- scope
- years covered
- databases/sources
- review methodology
- taxonomy
- number and type of comparison tables
- architectures/figures
- algorithms covered
- datasets/testbeds covered
- metrics covered
- reproducibility analysis
- standards analysis
- lessons learned
- open problems
- limitations

The final scope must demonstrate a clear need beyond existing surveys.
